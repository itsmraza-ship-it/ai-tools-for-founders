# AI Tools for Founders

A curated AI stack organised **by job, not by hype.**

Every week a new tool trends. Founders don't need 40 subscriptions — they need one reliable tool per job, reviewed by a human, at a cost they can explain. This guide maps the jobs every small company actually has, the mainstream tools that do them best, and what each tool really replaces.

> **Honest cost notes:** tools change pricing often, so this guide uses ranges, not invented precision. As a rule of thumb, most tools below have a free tier, and paid plans for individuals/small teams are roughly **$10–30/mo per tool**. Always check the vendor's current pricing page before budgeting.

## Principles First

1. **One tool per job.** Two tools doing the same job means you pay twice and master neither.
2. **Human review before anything public.** AI drafts; a human approves. Nothing client-facing, public, or financial goes out unreviewed.
3. **Never feed client secrets into random tools.** No client financials, IDs, passwords, or confidential documents into tools you haven't vetted. Use reputable vendors, check their data policies, and keep sensitive work in tools with proper business plans.
4. **Start free, upgrade on pain.** Begin on free tiers. Pay only when a limit is actively slowing real work.
5. **Replace tasks, not judgement.** AI is excellent at first drafts, summaries, and repetitive ops. Pricing, hiring, tax positions, and client advice stay human decisions.

## The Stack, By Job

### 1. Research & Answers
*The job: fast, sourced answers, market scans, and sense-making.*

| Tool | Best at | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Perplexity** | Quick research with cited sources — competitor scans, regulation lookups, market questions | Hours of tab-hopping and unverified forum answers |
| **ChatGPT** | Broad reasoning, brainstorming, drafting research plans, analysing documents you provide | A generalist research assistant for first passes |
| **Gemini** | Research inside the Google ecosystem; long documents; multimodal questions (images, docs) | Manual trawling through Drive files and long PDFs |
| **Claude** | Careful reading of long documents, nuanced summaries, clear structured analysis | Hours of reading contracts, guides, and reports line by line |

*Cost note:* all four offer free tiers; paid plans roughly $20/mo class.

### 2. Writing & Content
*The job: posts, emails, pages, and scripts — in your voice, at your pace.*

| Tool | Best at | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Claude** | Long-form writing that holds a tone — blogs, guides, thoughtful posts | A freelance writer's first-draft hours |
| **ChatGPT** | Versatile drafting, hooks, variations, repurposing one idea into many formats | Staring at a blank page; a junior copywriter for drafts |
| **Gemini** | Drafting and editing inside Google Docs/Gmail workflows | Copy-pasting between tools |
| **Notion AI** | Writing and summarising where your docs already live | Separate writing tools for internal docs |

*Cost note:* free tiers exist; expect roughly $10–20/mo for the writing layer you standardise on. Pick **one** primary writer — not three.

### 3. Design & Brand
*The job: social graphics, decks, and brand assets without a design department.*

| Tool | Best at | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Canva** | Social posts, carousels, decks, one-pagers — templates + brand kit, extremely fast | A design agency retainer for everyday assets |
| **Adobe Express** | Quick branded graphics inside the Adobe ecosystem | Back-and-forth with a designer for small jobs |
| **Figma** | Product/UI design and collaborative design files when you outgrow templates | Expensive design hand-offs for app/web work |

*Cost note:* Canva has a strong free tier; paid team plans roughly $10–15/mo per person class. This category replaces the most agency money per dollar spent.

### 4. Video
*The job: short-form clips, explainers, and edited talking-head content.*

| Tool | Best at | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **CapCut** | Fast short-form editing, captions, templates — phone and desktop | A video editor for routine reels/shorts |
| **Descript** | Edit video by editing the transcript; clean up podcasts and explainers | Hours of timeline editing for speech-based video |
| **Opus Clip** | Turning long videos into short clips automatically (review every clip before posting) | Manually hunting for clip-worthy moments |

*Cost note:* free tiers exist on all three; paid roughly $10–30/mo. AI clips are drafts — check captions and cuts before anything posts.

### 5. Bookkeeping & Finance Ops
*The job: clean books, invoices, and a clear view of cash — AI assists, it does not file your taxes.*

| Tool | Best at | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **QuickBooks Online** | Core bookkeeping, invoicing, bank feeds, and reporting for small companies | Spreadsheet bookkeeping and receipt shoeboxes |
| **Xero** | Bookkeeping with strong bank reconciliation and a large app ecosystem, popular outside the US | Manual reconciliation hours |
| **Wave** | Simple invoicing and accounting for very small businesses (free tier) | Paid software before you need it |

*Cost note:* this is software, not just AI — entry plans are roughly in the $15–30/mo class, rising with features. Use AI features (categorisation, matching) to speed up a human-reviewed process. **A qualified accountant still reviews and files.**

### 6. Customer Support
*The job: fast, consistent answers to the questions clients ask every week.*

| Tool | Best at | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Intercom** | Support inbox with AI-assisted replies and help-centre automation | A support hire for repetitive tier-1 questions |
| **Zendesk** | Structured ticketing for growing support volume | Email chaos across shared inboxes |
| **ChatGPT / Claude** (as drafting assistants) | Drafting replies in your tone for a human to send | Writing the same answer from scratch 50 times |

*Cost note:* support platforms are usually priced per seat, roughly $20+/mo per seat class. Start with AI-drafted replies in your existing inbox before buying a platform.

### 7. Coding & Automation
*The job: connect your tools, kill repetitive ops, and ship small software without a dev team.*

| Tool | Best at | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Zapier** | Simple, reliable automations between common apps (form → sheet → email) | VA hours spent copying data between tools |
| **Make** | More complex, visual automations at a lower cost per run | Custom dev work for multi-step workflows |
| **Claude / ChatGPT** (for code) | Writing and explaining scripts, formulas, and small tools | Waiting on a developer for every small fix |
| **GitHub Copilot** | Code completion inside your editor when you build regularly | Repetitive boilerplate typing |

*Cost note:* Zapier and Make both have free tiers; paid plans scale with usage, roughly $20/mo class at entry. Automate one painful workflow at a time — see [tool-stack-by-job.md](tool-stack-by-job.md) for the order.

### 8. Meetings & Notes
*The job: never lose a decision, action item, or client detail again.*

| Tool | Best at | Replaces |
|---|---|---|
| **Notion** | Meeting notes, wikis, and project docs in one searchable home | Scattered docs and "where did we write that down?" |
| **Fireflies.ai** | Automatic meeting transcription and searchable call summaries | Manual note-taking on calls |
| **Otter.ai** | Live transcription for meetings and interviews | Typing notes while trying to listen |
| **Granola** | Lightweight AI meeting notes that enhance your own notes | Post-call write-up time |

*Cost note:* free tiers exist; paid roughly $10–20/mo per user class. **Always tell call participants a tool is recording/transcribing.**

## Putting It Together

A lean founder stack is usually **6–8 tools total**, not 25:

- 1 research tool · 1 writing tool · Canva · 1 video tool · your accounting platform · Zapier or Make · 1 notes/meeting tool

The compact table, a starter stack on a modest budget, and the "automate first" order live in [tool-stack-by-job.md](tool-stack-by-job.md).
